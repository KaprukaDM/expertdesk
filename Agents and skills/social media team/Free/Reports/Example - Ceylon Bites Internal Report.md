## Step 0 — Industry Filter Classification

**Archetype locked: Food & Beverage**

Rationale: physical café/bakery location, menu-driven business, primary customer action is "come in / order," not a booking or a purchase-online flow. Closest secondary fit would be Service-Based Business (if they pivoted to catering-by-appointment), but F&B is the correct primary lens.

**What this locks in for the rest of the study:**
- Expected content pillars: menu items, behind-the-kitchen process, specials, table/atmosphere shots
- Missing Functions to check: order/delivery link, table-reservation link, menu link-in-bio
- Typical CTA: "Order now," "Reserve a table"
- Competitor set: nearby venues in the same cuisine/price bracket — locked to **Colombo Coffee Co.** (3 blocks away, direct competitor), **Cinnamon Cafe Union Place** (comparable price tier, 1.2km away), **Bakehouse Kollupitiya** (bakery-forward, adjacent category)

## 2. Industry Audit

**F&B benchmark context (Colombo café/bakery segment, similar follower range 2k–6k):**

- Typical posting cadence for accounts actually growing in this segment: 4-6 posts/week combined across formats, with at least 2 Reels/week
- Ceylon Bites is currently at ~1 post/week — well under half the segment's active-grower cadence
- Benchmark engagement rate for this segment/size: 2.0–3.5%. Ceylon Bites' visible engagement (likes+comments/followers on last 12 posts) estimates to **~1.4%** — below benchmark
- Seasonal pattern relevant here: Sri Lankan school-holiday periods (April, August, December) show a 20-30% uptick in café foot traffic regionally; no evidence Ceylon Bites has ever run seasonal-specific content
- Platform fit: Instagram and Facebook are correct primary platforms for this category in this market — no gap on platform choice itself

## 3. Competitor Analysis Audit

### Colombo Coffee Co. (primary competitor, 3 blocks away)
- Posts **daily**, 70% Reels (latte art, roastery process), 20% Stories, 10% static
- Highest-engagement post in the last 90 days: a 20-second "day in the life of a barista" Reel — 4,100 likes, 210 comments. This is a format Ceylon Bites has never attempted.
- Runs Meta ads Fri–Sun only, always the same static discount promo creative — a stale, easy-to-outclass ad format
- Weakness: almost no bakery/food content, entirely coffee-focused — leaves food-forward content as open ground

### Cinnamon Cafe Union Place
- Posts 3x/week, heavier on static plating shots, high production value
- Strong on table/atmosphere content — clearly invests in a photographer
- Weakness: captions are generic ("Delicious!" "Yum!") — low value content, no hooks, no CTAs. Comments are mostly emoji, low genuine engagement despite decent likes

### Bakehouse Kollupitiya
- Posts 2x/week, bread/pastry process videos perform best (unfiltered, phone-shot, clearly resonates)
- Runs a weekly "today's bake" Story series — simple, consistent, drives repeat visibility
- Weakness: no Reels at all, missing the highest-reach format entirely

**Open ground identified:** No competitor in this set is doing consistent baking-process Reels *combined* with genuine food-forward (not just coffee) content. This is Ceylon Bites' single clearest opportunity.

## 4. Content Audit

Reviewed last 24 Instagram posts (approx. 6 months of history at current low cadence) and last 20 Facebook posts.

- **Pillars present:** Product/menu shots (60% of posts), occasional team photos (15%)
- **Pillars absent:** No process/BTS content, no customer UGC/testimonials, no seasonal/trend content, no Q&A-style captions
- **Format mix:** 85% static posts, 10% carousel, 5% Reels (1 Reel in the last 6 months) — heavily over-indexed on static
- **Value vs. sales ratio:** ~90% of captions are directly promotional ("Try our new croissant! 🥐 Order now") — almost no educational/entertaining/informational content
- **Raw vs. polished:** Photos are consistently well-lit and styled (slightly on the polished side) — not a negative in itself, but the complete absence of any raw/native-feeling content (the format that's outperforming for competitors) is the gap
- **Content about making content:** Zero — no process shots of baking, brewing, or prep at all
- **Trend participation:** No evidence of trending-audio or format participation in the sample reviewed
- **Posting frequency:** ~1x/week, declining slightly over the last 3 months (was ~1.3x/week 6 months ago)
- **Brand consistency:** Strong — warm terracotta tones, consistent filter, on-brand across both platforms. One of the account's genuine strengths.
- **Caption grammar/clarity:** Clean, no errors
- **Hashtags:** 3-5 generic hashtags per post (#colombofood #srilanka #bakery) — no niche or branded hashtag, no location-specific tags beyond "Colombo"
- **Image quality:** Good — consistent lighting, no blur/quality issues
- **Image formats — sizing audit against best practice, checked post by post:** reviewed all 24 Instagram posts against the correct spec per placement (feed **1080×1350** portrait — the recommended default — with 1080×1080 square acceptable but suboptimal; Stories & Reels 1080×1920). 18 of 24 feed posts use the recommended 1080×1350; the remaining 6 are square 1080×1080 — not wrong, but leaving screen real estate (and the reach that comes with it) on the table. **2 posts flagged as actual errors**: the Jul 14 carousel's second slide is a stretched 4:5 crop of a source image shot at 1:1, visibly distorting the pastry shape; the single existing Reel was exported at 1080×1080 (square) instead of 1080×1920, so it displays letterboxed in the Reels tab — actively hurting its already-weak performance further. No stretching found on Facebook uploads.
- **Logo placement:** N/A — logo not overlaid on content images, only used in profile picture (not a finding either way)
- **Reels frame structure:** The single existing Reel opens with a 4-second static establishing shot before any action — buried hook, would likely lose viewers before the interesting part (the actual bake) appears
- **Subtitles:** The one Reel has no burned-in captions
- **Best-performing post (all-time, of reviewed sample):** A carousel of a new seasonal menu item — 312 likes, 3.2x the account's average. Hypothesis: novelty + carousel format (more surface area) outperformed single static
- **Worst-performing:** A generic "we're open today" post with a stock coffee-cup graphic — 41 likes. Hypothesis: no product/photo, no reason to engage
- **Scent congruency:** Bio and captions never mention delivery, but the Facebook page does list a delivery partner logo in a photo — inconsistent messaging across surfaces

## 8. What's Missing — Synthesis

Pulling Gap Audit (§5) and Missing Functions Audit (§7) together: a fully set-up F&B competitor in this exact market has (1) a working order/delivery link in bio, (2) at least weekly Reels of the baking/prep process, (3) a Stories cadence for daily visibility, (4) NAP-consistent, correctly-categorized profiles on every platform, and (5) some amount of paid amplification behind proven organic content. Ceylon Bites currently has **none** of these five, despite having the single strongest visual brand identity of the three competitors reviewed.

## 9. SWOT Analysis

**Strengths**
- Strong, consistent visual brand identity (warm terracotta palette, on-brand across platforms) — genuinely better than any competitor reviewed
- Well-organized Instagram highlights structure
- NAP consistency between Instagram and Facebook (address/hours match)
- Clean, error-free caption writing

**Weaknesses**
- Posting cadence (~1x/week) less than half the segment's active-grower benchmark
- Dead link-in-bio — actively losing whatever click-intent the bio generates
- No CTA button on Instagram; generic "Send Message" on Facebook
- Facebook page miscategorized as "Community" instead of "Bakery," hurting discovery
- Zero Reels/process content despite it being the single highest-performing format in the competitor set
- 90% promotional captions, no value/educational content
- Zero paid amplification

**Opportunities**
- No competitor combines food-forward content with consistent baking-process Reels — wide open positioning
- Seasonal school-holiday traffic upticks are un-targeted by any competitor's content calendar
- Best-performing own post (seasonal-item carousel) suggests carousels + novelty already work here — an existing format to build on, not just a new one to learn

**Threats**
- Colombo Coffee Co., 3 blocks away, posts daily and already runs weekend paid ads — actively growing distance in cadence and paid presence
- Continued below-benchmark engagement risks the account becoming algorithmically less favored over time (compounding, not static, risk)
