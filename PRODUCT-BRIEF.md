# Product & Copy Brief — Expert Desk

*Reference doc for anyone writing copy (ads, landing pages, WhatsApp, social) for this product. Product name: "Expert Desk" (expertdesk.lk).*

---

## 1. What this is, in one line

**Agency-quality digital marketing, delivered by real experts, at fixed prices — without the retainer, the wait, or the account-manager relay.**

A productized marketing services storefront, owned and operated by **Kapruka's Digital Marketing team**. Think "Fiverr-style packages" (Basic / Standard / Premium, fixed scope, fixed price, fixed delivery time) but every service is fulfilled in-house by a vetted specialist — not an open marketplace of anonymous freelancers.

## 2. Who it's for

**Sri Lankan SMEs** — shop owners, small brands, and growing local businesses who need real marketing work done but can't justify (or don't trust) a traditional agency retainer. They're price-sensitive, time-poor, and have been burned before by slow, vague, or overpriced agency engagements.

## 3. The problem we solve

Hiring a marketing agency is painful in three specific ways — and the product exists to kill all three:

| Pain | Agency reality | Our answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Time** | Weeks of onboarding calls before anything ships | Pick a package, brief it in one form, work starts the same week |
| **Cost** | A retainer from day one, whether you need 40 hours or 4 | One fixed price per package — pay for the deliverable, not the overhead |
| **Communication** | Your brief passes through an account manager before it reaches whoever does the work | You talk straight to the expert doing your work, every time |

**The one-line pitch to keep coming back to:** *everything an agency promises, at about half the price, with none of the wait, the markup, or the middlemen.*

## 4. What we sell — 10 services, one consistent ladder

Every service below is sold in the same **Basic / Standard / Premium** ladder — customers learn the pattern once and it works everywhere. What changes per service is the scope, price, delivery time and revisions — not the shape of the decision.

| Service | One-liner (use for cards/ads) |
|---|---|
| **SEO** | Rank higher, get found by people already searching for you |
| **CRO** (Conversion Rate Optimization) | Turn more of your existing traffic into paying customers |
| **Google Ads** | Search campaigns that spend on people ready to buy |
| **Meta Ads** | Facebook & Instagram campaigns built to convert, not just reach |
| **Content Strategy** | A social content plan and calendar built around your brand |
| **Video Creation** | Scroll-stopping video made for your product and audience |
| **Graphic Design** | Branding, ads and social creative that looks agency-made |
| **GEO — AI Search** | Get cited by ChatGPT, Gemini and AI search results *(always spell out "AI Search" — GEO alone is jargon nobody's heard of)* |
| **Video Editing** | Raw footage turned into polished, ready-to-post video |

**Package tier shape (same across every service):**
- **Basic** — one focused deliverable, fast, single revision — **50 tokens**
- **Standard** *(most popular)* — full scope for the service, 2 revisions, priority turnaround — **200 tokens**
- **Premium** — recurring monthly engagement, unlimited revisions in scope, dedicated specialist — **500 tokens/mo**

Pricing is denominated in **tokens**, not LKR — one fungible currency that works the same across every service, topped up in the wallet. The token cost is **uniform across all 10 services** (50/200/500 regardless of which service you pick) — copy should never imply one service costs more tokens than another.

**Note on naming — two systems, by design:** the public marketing site uses "Basic / Standard / Premium" as the go-to-market label for this ladder. The logged-in product (the real working audit tool, covering a related but not identical 7-department set — SEO, GEO, Google Ads, Meta Ads, Content Strategist, UI/UX, CRO) uses its own names for the same 50/200/500-token ladder: **"Quick Check" / "Full Audit + Plan" / "Growth Partner."** Both are intentional — don't try to force them into a single label set. When writing copy for the public/ad-facing side, use Basic/Standard/Premium; when writing in-product copy (dashboard, emails about a specific audit run, etc.), use Quick Check/Full Audit + Plan/Growth Partner.

## 5. Voice & tone

Two layers apply, and both matter:

**Kapruka's official brand tone** (from the Kapruka Brand Guidelines): *Premium, Warm, Trustworthy, Celebratory, Friendly.* Polished and confident, never cold or corporate; never distant or overly formal.

**This product's specific voice**, layered on top: **blunt, plain-language, no-jargon, slightly contrarian toward "agency" as a category.** Short sentences. Concrete numbers over vague claims. Em-dashes for punchy contrast (see the hero line above). Never hide a number without translating it into money, time, or an outcome the reader actually cares about.

**Words to avoid:** meta description, H1, schema, canonical, CTR, bounce rate, SERP, impressions, alt text, sitemap, and any other jargon a shop owner wouldn't use out loud. If a service name itself is jargon (like "GEO"), spell out what it means immediately next to it.

## 6. Brand identity

- **Primary color:** `#008060` (green) — deliberately departs from Kapruka's house purple; chosen as this product's own palette rather than inherited.
- **Accent/secondary:** `#95BF47` (bright green), `#5E8E3E` (olive), `#002D2D` (deep teal — also the dark-mode canvas)
- **Neutrals:** white, black
- **Typeface:** Inter (mandatory, per Kapruka brand guidelines) — do not introduce other display fonts
- **Relationship framing:** this product is **"Powered by Kapruka"** — it's a Kapruka-owned tool/service, not the main Kapruka.com storefront. Don't imply it *is* the main e-commerce site; do lean on Kapruka's trust and scale as a credibility signal (23 years in business, Sri Lanka's largest locally-owned e-commerce company) if it's useful in longer-form copy.

## 8. Guardrails — things NOT to fabricate in copy

This product is pre-launch with no real customers yet. Do not write copy that implies otherwise:

- **No fake reviews, testimonials, or star ratings.** Don't invent "500+ happy clients" or quote a customer that doesn't exist.
- **No fake client logos** ("trusted by X, Y, Z") unless those are real, confirmed clients.
- **No fake specialist personas** (invented names, headshots, "127 orders completed"). Frame delivery as "a specialist from our in-house team," not a named individual with fabricated stats.
- Illustrative example numbers (like the sample "popular packages" card on the homepage) are fine **only if clearly labeled as an example**, matching the existing pattern on the site.

## 9. Positioning note / history

An earlier internal doc (`sme-marketing-copilot-architecture.md`) describes a *different* concept for this same project name: a self-serve, DIY audit-and-coach tool (audit → rupee-ranked findings → guided self-fixes → WhatsApp check-ins), with **no human experts doing the work for you.** That direction has been superseded by the done-for-you expert marketplace described in this brief. If you see old copy or docs referencing "audit," "fix it yourself," or "WhatsApp coaching" as the core loop, treat it as outdated — the current product sells finished work, not DIY guidance.
